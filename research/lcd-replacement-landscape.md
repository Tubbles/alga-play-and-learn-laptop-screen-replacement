# Research: toy-laptop LCD replacement landscape

Web research performed by a Claude research agent, 2026-07-31. Claims are cited with URLs; items marked *assessment* or *inference* are the agent's engineering judgment, not sourced fact. Kept verbatim below (light formatting only) as raw reference for [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md).

**Device context (verified):** The current Alga Laptop SE/FI is sold as a kids' computer for ages 3–6 with a "6-inch LCD screen with backlight" ("6″ LCD-skärm med bakgrundsbelysning"), 45 activities, 3 difficulty levels, powered by 3×AAA ([babyland.se product page](https://www.babyland.se/alga_laptop_sefi)). Alga is the Swedish games brand acquired by BRIO in 1983, both owned by Ravensburger since 2015 ([Wikipedia: Alga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alga_(game_publisher)), [Ravensburger press release](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/ravensburger-acquires-renowned-swedish-toy-company-brio-287891001.html)). *Inference, not verified:* a 3×AAA power budget and the toy-laptop price class strongly suggest a monochrome passive-matrix (STN/FSTN or TN) dot-matrix panel with LED backlight, made by an anonymous Chinese OEM, not a color TFT — but this is exactly what opening the unit must confirm. No Alga-specific teardown or repair writeup was found anywhere (searched in English and Swedish).

## 1. How toy-laptop screens are constructed, and what a hobbyist can service

General engineering knowledge, with sources where noted. Cheap educational laptops fall into a few construction patterns:

- **Custom mono passive glass driven directly by a COB "blob" ASIC** on the mainboard. The epoxy blob gives no clue what chip is underneath ([Hackaday tag page discussing COB blobs](https://hackaday.com/tag/logic-analyzer/), [EEVblog thread on COB repair](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/repair/repair-cob-(blob)-electronics/)). Connection to the glass is one of:
  - **Zebra strip / elastomeric connector**: alternating conductive/insulating rubber layers clamped between PCB pads and ITO pads on the glass; alignment and even clamping pressure are critical ([electro-tech-online explanation](https://www.electro-tech-online.com/threads/elastomeric-connectors.110678/), [badcaps thread](https://www.badcaps.net/forum/troubleshooting-hardware-devices-and-electronics-theory/general-electronics-technical-discussion/68022-repair-lcd-display-contact-rubber)). **Hobbyist-serviceable: yes** — disassemble, clean glass and strip with alcohol, reseat; worn strips can sometimes be rejuvenated or replaced with generic elastomeric stock ([electronics-lab thread](https://www.electronics-lab.com/forums/threads/can-you-fix-a-zebra-strip.256566/), [dslreports thread](https://www.dslreports.com/forum/r31661458-how-to-fix-a-zebra)).
  - **Heat-seal / TAB flex bonded with anisotropic conductive adhesive (ACF)** to glass and/or PCB. **Hobbyist-serviceable: marginal.** Failed bonds cause dead rows/columns and can *sometimes* be revived by re-flowing the bond with controlled heat and pressure (hot-air + pencil eraser, chisel-tip iron, hot bar) — but the ACF is thermosetting, so re-melting is unreliable, ~150 °C is needed, overheat kills the LCD, and proper re-bonding needs an ACF hot-bar machine ([Hackaday heat-seal repair writeup + comments](https://hackaday.com/2009/06/20/repair-a-malfunctioning-lcd/), [All About Circuits heat-seal thread](https://forum.allaboutcircuits.com/threads/lcd-heat-seal-coming-off-suggestions.179326/), [edaboard flex repair thread](https://www.edaboard.com/threads/lcd-flex-ribbon-repair.220248/)). Full re-bonding of a *detached* flex is effectively a specialist job.
- **COG module (controller chip on the glass) with a flex tail** to a mainboard connector or soldered/heat-sealed to it. If the flex plugs into a connector, the whole module is a swappable unit — **serviceable if an identical module can be sourced**. Modern retail COG graphic modules of this style are commodity items ([Winstar 256×128 COG](https://www.winstar.com.tw/products/lcd-display/cog-lcd-module-display/256x128-lcd.html), [Raystar RX256128A](https://www.raystar-optronics.com/graphic-lcd-display-module/ST75256.html)).
- **Custom segment glass** (fixed icons/characters, not a dot matrix — typical for the youngest age bracket). Broken glass is unobtainable off the shelf; the only documented fix is having new glass custom-manufactured, as Robert Baruch did for the Sharp PC-1211: a Chinese LCD maker charged ~$300 for tooling + 10 samples, ~$800 total for 210 pieces ([Hackaday: Designing custom LCDs to repair retrocomputers](https://hackaday.com/2019/03/03/designing-custom-lcds-to-repair-retrocomputers/), [EEVblog design thread](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/projects/lcd-design-is-this-good-enough/)).

**Bottom line (assessment):** zebra-strip reseating and whole-module swaps are hobbyist territory; heat-seal re-flow is a coin-flip; heat-seal re-bonding and broken custom glass are effectively unrepairable without sourcing new glass or custom manufacture.

## 2. Failure modes and distinguishing symptoms

- **Cracked/ruptured cell — "ink blots"/spreading dark patches, spider cracks.** Pressure or impact ruptures the cell; liquid crystal bleeds and the damage *spreads over time*, unlike stable glass-only cracks. Not repairable; needs new glass/module ([everythingmobilelimited: cracked glass vs broken LCD](https://everythingmobilelimited.com/cracked-glass-vs-broken-lcd/), [risinglcd on spot damage](https://www.risinglcd.com/news/LCD-Screen-Spot-Damage-%E2%80%93-Causes-Standards-Diagnosis-and-Prevention.html), [itechpost on ink-blot damage](https://www.itechpost.com/articles/106606/20210805/samsung-screen-bleeding-what-phone-ink-blot-pixel-damage.htm)).
- **Missing rows/columns or fading segments — connection fault**, i.e. zebra strip contact loss or failing heat-seal bond. The Hackaday heat-seal technique explicitly targets "dead rows and columns of pixels" ([Hackaday 2009](https://hackaday.com/2009/06/20/repair-a-malfunctioning-lcd/)); zebra faults respond to cleaning/reseating ([badcaps](https://www.badcaps.net/forum/troubleshooting-hardware-devices-and-electronics-theory/general-electronics-technical-discussion/68022-repair-lcd-display-contact-rubber), [YBW instrument LCD thread](https://forums.ybw.com/threads/repairing-lcd-screens-on-electronic-instruments.293234/)). Rule of thumb (general knowledge): symptoms that change when you squeeze the bezel point at zebra/bond, not glass.
- **Damaged/degraded polarizer — washed-out, dark, or "sunburned" image on an otherwise working panel.** The polarizer is a plastic film glued to the glass and is replaceable; the Game Boy scene has made this routine, with cheap replacement film sold off the shelf ([Hackaday: repairing a sunburned Game Boy screen](https://hackaday.com/2018/01/26/repairing-a-sunburned-game-boy-screen/), [Hand Held Legend polarization film](https://handheldlegend.com/products/polarization-film)). Caveat from the same source: mono panels use *linear* film, color LCDs use *circular* film — they are not interchangeable.
- **Dead backlight — screen dark but content faintly visible at an angle or under a flashlight.** General engineering knowledge; for toy laptops the backlight is usually a couple of side-firing LEDs plus a light guide, and iFixit's VTech troubleshooting treats "display light does not turn on → replace LEDs" as a standard repair ([iFixit VTech Baby's Learning Laptop troubleshooting](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/VTech_Baby%27s_Learning_Laptop_Troubleshooting)).

## 3. Sourcing an identical replacement panel

- **Identification (well-established practice):** look for silkscreened part numbers on the module PCB, the flex tail, and the edge of the glass itself. For commodity modules the PCB number (e.g. `LMG7420`, `320240A`) is the search key — an eBay listing for a Hitachi `LMG7420`/`LMG7410` 5.1″ 240×128 module shows exactly this pattern ([eBay LMG7420 listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/203146522022)). The same convention holds for real laptop panels (`LP…`, `LTN…` printed on the back) ([computerhope guide](https://www.computerhope.com/issues/ch001184.htm)). *Caution (general knowledge):* toy OEM glass often carries only an internal number and a date code; that number is still worth searching verbatim on AliExpress/Taobao, since the same OEM glass ships in many badge-engineered toys.
- **Where to look:** AliExpress and eBay for both modules and bare glass; made-in-china.com B2B listings ([example ST75256 module listing](https://reaper88.en.made-in-china.com/product/XysmOHEunPVz/China-Monochrome-Graphic-LCD-Display-Module-Cog-256X128-Dots-with-FSTN-Positive-Glass-Controller-St75256.html)); Taobao via a forwarding agent for China-domestic-only parts (general knowledge — no specific listing verified); Western distributors for standard modules: [Newhaven](https://newhavendisplay.com/320x240-graphic-lcd-module-stn-blue-display-with-white-backlight-negative-voltage/), [Crystalfontz](https://www.crystalfontz.com/product/cfag320240c0ymitz), [BuyDisplay](https://www.buydisplay.com/5-7-inch-320x240-lcd-controller-sed1335-or-equivalent-white-on-blue), Winstar, Raystar. My LCSC search surfaced no meaningful mono-graphic-module depth; treat LCSC as a long shot for this (weakly sourced — absence of results, not positive evidence).
- **Panelook:** it is overwhelmingly a TFT panel database, but it *does* index some monochrome STN graphic modules — e.g. a [TVG12232B 122×32 STN module detail page](http://www.panelook.com/TVG12232B-STN-STN-122x32-Monochrome-LCD-Graphic-Display-Module-detail_71885.html). However, its inventory/broker ecosystem is TFT-centric, and an anonymous toy OEM panel will not be listed. Useful only if the Alga turns out to contain a branded standard module (or a TFT). Note: panelook sits behind a slider-CAPTCHA, which limits scripted searching.
- **Custom remanufacture** as last resort for broken custom glass: viable, ~$300–800 and a datasheet-grade spec needed ([Hackaday PC-1211 story](https://hackaday.com/2019/03/03/designing-custom-lcds-to-repair-retrocomputers/)). Realistic for segment glass; a 6″ dot-matrix glass would additionally need the drive spec to match the blob ASIC, making this much harder (assessment).

## 4. Generic retrofit options if the exact panel is unobtainable

**Available ~5–7″ generic modules (all verified product listings):**

- **Mono STN/FSTN 320×240, 5.7″, RA8835/SED1335 controller, 8-bit parallel** — the workhorse class: [BuyDisplay 5.7″ SED1335](https://www.buydisplay.com/5-7-inch-320x240-lcd-controller-sed1335-or-equivalent-white-on-blue), [Newhaven NHD-320240WG (RA8835)](https://newhavendisplay.com/320x240-graphic-lcd-module-stn-blue-display-with-white-backlight-negative-voltage/), [~$30-class eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/203143156162)/[Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/320240-320x240-320240A-Controller-160x109mm/dp/B0CWZ55KY9) imports, [Raystar RX320240E COG variant](https://www.raystar-optronics.com/graphic-lcd-display-module/RX320240E.html). RA8835 is the dominant controller in this class today; T6963C survives mainly in the 240×128 (~5.1″, LMG7420-class) tier.
- **6″ 320×240 STN, NT7086 controller** ([displaymodule.com 6-inch](https://www.displaymodule.com/products/320x240-lcd-graphic-display-module-6-inch-with-mcu)) — dimensionally closest to the Alga's 6″ screen.
- **Mono COG ST75256-class (SPI/I2C/parallel)** — only up to ~3″ / 256×128 ([Winstar](https://www.winstar.com.tw/products/lcd-display/cog-lcd-module-display/256x128-lcd.html), [Raystar](https://www.raystar-optronics.com/graphic-lcd-display-module/ST75256.html)); too small for a 6″ bay, listed for completeness. u8g2 supports this whole controller family ([u8g2 gallery](https://github.com/olikraus/u8g2/wiki/gallery)).
- **Color TFTs 5–7″** with SPI/parallel/RGB or HDMI driver boards are ubiquitous (general knowledge; the PinkPad build below used a 5″ HDMI touchscreen).

**Driving them from the toy mainboard — the hard truth (assessment grounded in the sources above):** if the toy's blob ASIC drives raw glass directly (multiplexed row/column waveforms, no controller protocol), there is *nothing to plug a generic module into* — the "interface" is analog drive voltages matched to that specific glass geometry. If the toy instead talks a controller protocol (as in the VTech PreComputer 2000, which uses an HD44780 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTech_PreComputer_2000))), a retrofit is possible only if the replacement speaks the same protocol and tolerates the toy's init sequence. Determining which case applies requires probing: sigrok/PulseView-style logic-analyzer capture of the flex ([Hackaday logic-analyzer series](https://hackaday.com/series_of_posts/logic-analyzers/), [decoding/monitoring article](https://hackaday.com/2024/04/15/logic-analyzers-decoding-and-monitoring/)). Precedent for the "protocol-bridge" approach: Kerry Wong captured a Uniden cordless-phone LCD's traffic, identified a KS0713-type protocol, and re-displayed it via a microcontroller onto a modern SPI LCD ([writeup](http://www.kerrywong.com/2017/06/04/reverse-engineering-a-uniden-cordlessphone-lcd/) — site was unreachable during this session; characterization from search-result summaries, lower confidence). The pragmatic alternative is the **brain transplant**: keep the shell/keyboard, discard the mainboard, fit a Pi + generic display — losing the original educational firmware. Done repeatedly: [PinkPad — VTech shell + 5″ touchscreen + Raspberry Pi + Arch Linux ARM](https://hackaday.com/2026/05/13/vtech-toy-becomes-pinkpad-the-diy-linux-laptop/) (project page: missmolerat.com/posts/pinkpad/), [Labz Max Steel toy computers + Pi + larger screens](https://hackaday.com/2022/09/30/this-computer-is-definitely-not-a-toy/).

**Practical implication for ranking (assessment):** the decision tree hinges entirely on what the teardown reveals — (a) symptom is zebra/bond-related → free fix, try first; (b) intact module with a part number → hunt AliExpress/eBay/Taobao verbatim; (c) broken custom glass driven raw by a blob → realistic options collapse to donor unit (second-hand Alga Laptop, likely the cheapest and most faithful path) or brain transplant; protocol capture is only worth attempting if a discrete LCD controller protocol exists on the flex.
